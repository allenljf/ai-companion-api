# Travel DNA Design System v3.2

> Master Design System for KKday Travel DNA Social Cards

## Objective

Generate one premium travel personality social sharing card.

Do **not** design a new poster. Treat every output as another edition of
the same collectible travel postcard series.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Required Inputs

Every generation always includes:

1.  **Design System (.md)**
2.  **Composition Reference Image**
3.  **Result JSON**

## Priority

1.  Design System
2.  Composition Reference Image
3.  Result JSON

The **Composition Reference Image** defines the layout only.

The **Design System** defines the visual language, composition rules and
generation behavior.

The **Result JSON** provides the dynamic content.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Design Philosophy

-   Maintain an existing product design system.
-   Consistency is more important than layout creativity.
-   Creativity should be expressed only through the Hero Illustration
    and destination-localized elements.
-   Imagine updating content inside a locked Figma template.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Four-Layer Design System

## Layer 1 --- Layout (Locked)

The Composition Reference Image is a **wireframe**, not an illustration
reference.

It defines only:

-   Overall composition
-   Visual hierarchy
-   White space
-   Component positions
-   Component proportions
-   Hero / Information Card relationship
-   Companion Postcard placement
-   Branding placement
-   Marketing area placement

Never redesign or reinterpret the layout.

### Placeholder Slots (Critical)

The wireframe contains placeholder markers: grey blocks, dashed
outlines, generic image icons, and labels such as "YOUR ILLUSTRATION
HERE" or "PLACEHOLDER".

These markers indicate **content slots only**. They must **NEVER**
appear in the final output --- no grey boxes, no dashed borders, no
generic image icons, no placeholder text of any kind.

Every placeholder slot must be filled with fully rendered, finished
content appropriate to the destination and personality:

-   The large top area → the Hero Illustration (full-bleed, finished
    artwork).
-   The three small boxes above the hashtags → three finished icon
    illustrations, one per hashtag, each visually matching its hashtag
    meaning and localized to the destination.
-   The bottom-left and bottom-right placeholder boxes → localized
    decorative sticker artwork (see Layer 3).
-   The QR block → keep as a scannable-looking QR code graphic in the
    same position.

If any slot would be left empty, fill it with destination-appropriate
decorative artwork instead --- never leave wireframe graphics visible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Layer 2 --- Hero Illustration (Creative)

Create a completely new Hero Illustration every generation.

Do NOT imitate the reference image's:

-   Camera angle
-   Viewpoint
-   Character pose
-   Framing
-   Subject arrangement
-   Foreground composition

Instead answer:

> "If this travel personality visited this destination, what memorable
> moment would they experience?"

Prioritize environmental storytelling over landmark showcase.

### Hero Composition Rules

-   The final canvas is **9:16**.
-   The center **4:5** region is the Safe Area.
-   Assume Instagram Feed and Threads crop away the top and bottom.
-   Place the visual focus inside the 4:5 Safe Area.
-   Position the visual focus slightly lower than the vertical center of
    the full canvas.
-   Keep the traveler's face, body and important storytelling inside the
    Safe Area.
-   Avoid placing important subjects near the very top or bottom.

Upper Story Extension should mainly contain:

-   Sky
-   Clouds
-   Atmosphere
-   Lighting
-   Mountains
-   Skyline

Lower Story Extension should smoothly transition into the marketing
section without containing important storytelling.

The cropped 4:5 Safe Area should still feel like a complete standalone
composition.

### Character Scale (Critical)

The traveler must be depicted as a **small figure within a grand
environment** --- roughly **half the height** of a typical human-scale
subject in this kind of illustration. The environment, not the
character, is the visual protagonist.

Concretely:

-   The character's full body height should occupy no more than
    **15--20% of the frame's vertical height** (a small, wide, or
    establishing shot, not a portrait or medium close-up).
-   Never crop the character at the waist or chest to make them appear
    larger --- the character should be shown at full or near-full body
    scale, simply positioned further back within a spacious scene.
-   Surround the character with generous negative space, environment
    detail, and depth (foreground, midground, background) so the scene
    reads as a wide environmental shot.
-   Do NOT fill the frame with the character. Do NOT use a close-up,
    portrait, or medium shot. Avoid the character's body or face
    dominating more than a small fraction of the composition.
-   The face must still read as clearly visible and recognizable at
    this smaller scale --- keep enough resolution and clarity on the
    face even though the character occupies less of the frame.

### Character Guidelines

The traveler should feel naturally integrated into the destination
rather than posing for the viewer.

**Gender (Required Variation):** Randomly choose the traveler's gender
--- male or female --- independently for every generation, with roughly
equal probability. Do NOT default to male. The chosen gender must fit
the personality and scene naturally.

The character may be:

-   Walking through the destination
-   Exploring local streets
-   Interacting with local people
-   Enjoying local food
-   Participating in local activities
-   Quietly observing the surroundings
-   Resting naturally within the environment

**Viewpoint (Strict):** The head and body must always read as one
anatomically coherent pose --- never force the head to face a different
direction than the body naturally would. The neck may rotate the head
relative to the shoulders/torso only within a natural, comfortable
range (no more than roughly 45--60° from the torso's facing direction).
Never twist the head against the body's rotation to fake a frontal or
visible face.

Choose ONE overall body orientation, and let the head follow it
naturally:

-   **Front view:** torso faces the camera or scene in front; head faces
    forward, or turns slightly (up to ~45°) to either side.
-   **Three-quarter view:** torso is angled roughly 30--45° away from the
    camera; head is angled to match, optionally turning a little further
    toward camera within the natural neck-rotation range.
-   **Side view:** torso is in full side profile; head is also in side
    profile (or turned only very slightly), never counter-rotated to
    face the camera.

The character's face must be clearly visible in every generation ---
achieve this by choosing a body orientation (front or three-quarter)
that naturally shows the face, never by twisting the head unnaturally
against a side- or rear-facing body.

If a pose or action (e.g., looking at a menu, bending down, reaching for
something, interacting with a vendor) would naturally turn the body away
from the camera, choose a body/head orientation combination (front or
three-quarter) where the face remains naturally visible, rather than
forcing the head to counter-rotate against the body.

**NEVER**:

-   Depict the character fully from behind, walking away, or with the
    body facing away from the camera while the head/face is forced to
    turn back toward the viewer --- this produces an anatomically
    impossible, unsettling neck twist.
-   Rotate the head beyond a natural range relative to the shoulders/
    torso facing direction.
-   Hide the face with hair, hats, objects, motion blur, or an unnatural
    angle.

Avoid overly staged poses or generic "standing and looking at a
landmark" compositions.

The character's facial expression, body language and interactions should
reinforce the travel personality.

Whenever appropriate, storytelling should come from the character's
actions rather than relying only on the scenery.

## Layer 3 --- Localized Elements

Regenerate these according to the destination:

-   Passport stamp artwork
-   Decorative plants
-   Decorative ornaments
-   Accent colors
-   Local textures
-   Local patterns
-   Supporting illustrations
-   Travel tickets
-   The three hashtag icon illustrations

The reference image defines only their placement.

Never copy destination-specific decorative assets from the reference
image.

Decorations should reflect the local culture and environment while
preserving the same visual weight and placement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------


## Layer 4 — Content (JSON)


Populate every text and hashtag slot only with the supplied JSON. Use the JSON values verbatim: never invent, translate, rephrase, shorten, or extend them. If a field has no value, leave that slot visually empty and fill it with decorative artwork (see Layer 1); never leave placeholder text.


### Content Blocks and Data Mapping


The Information Card contains two text groups, one companion note, and one hashtag row. Map each slot to its JSON field exactly as follows.


Group A — Personality (upper text block, left)

- Eyebrow label: fixed text "我的旅行人格". Static; a small label above the title.
- Personality name (main title): from JSON Personality. The largest text in this group; the dominant title.

Group B — Destination (lower text block, left)

- Eyebrow label: fixed text "我的命定旅程". Static; a small label above the destination.
- Destination (main title): from JSON Destination.
- Subtitle: from JSON Journey Copy. Sits directly below the Destination.

Group C — Companion Postcard (right note)

- Companion quote: from JSON Companion Quote. Rendered as a handwritten-style note on the torn-paper postcard.
- Signature: from the JSON companion name field, e.g. "— 你的旅伴 小K".
- Passport stamp: localized artwork (see Layer 3), containing the destination name and country in a localized stamp design.


Group D — Hashtag Row (three icons with labels)

- Hashtag 1: from JSON hashtags item 1, rendered with a leading "#".
- Hashtag 2: from JSON hashtags item 2, rendered with a leading "#".
- Hashtag 3: from JSON hashtags item 3, rendered with a leading "#".
- Three hashtag icons: localized icon illustrations (see Layer 3). One finished icon per hashtag, each icon's meaning matching its - - hashtag.

The three hashtags are dynamic (they change per result) and must never be hard-coded. Each icon must visually correspond to the meaning of its paired hashtag and be localized to the destination.


### Typography Hierarchy and Order (Locked)

Within each left-side text group, the vertical order and size hierarchy are fixed here (horizontal position still follows the reference image):

Eyebrow labels ("我的旅行人格", "我的命定旅程") are the smallest text, positioned above their respective titles as small labels.
Personality name and Destination are main titles — the largest text in their groups.
The Subtitle (Journey Copy) sits directly below the Destination and must be clearly one level smaller than the Destination. The size difference must be obvious at a glance, so the Destination reads as the dominant title and the Subtitle as secondary supporting text.

Top-to-bottom order in the left column: 我的旅行人格 → Personality name → 我的命定旅程 → Destination → Subtitle.

### Hashtag Row Rules

- Exactly three hashtags, evenly spaced in one row, aligned to the reference image positions.
- Each hashtag is centered with its own icon as a label-icon pair; keep the three pairs equal in visual weight and size.
- All three hashtag labels share the same size (smaller than the titles); do not emphasize any one over the others.

### Do Not

- Do not add, remove, or reorder content blocks.
- Do not change which JSON field feeds which slot.
- Do not make the Subtitle equal to or larger than the Destination.
- Do not hard-code hashtags or invent extra ones.
- Do not leave any eyebrow label, title, subtitle, or hashtag blank if its JSON value exists.

# Canvas

Master canvas: **9:16**

The Composition Reference Image represents only the central **4:5 Safe
Area**.

Always generate new Story Extensions above and below.

Upper extension: - Sky - Clouds - Atmosphere - Lighting

Lower extension: - CTA - KKday logo - QR placeholder

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Final Rule

If multiple cards are displayed side by side:

-   Layout should remain visually identical.
-   Hero Illustrations should all be unique.
-   Destination decorations should be localized.
-   Traveler genders and viewpoints should vary across cards (never all
    male, never all the same pose), while faces always remain visible.
-   The character should consistently occupy a small scale within a
    spacious environment across all cards (see Character Scale), never
    a close-up or medium shot on any single card.
-   Every card should instantly be recognized as belonging to the same
    Travel DNA product family.
-   No wireframe placeholder graphics or placeholder text may remain
    visible in any card.
